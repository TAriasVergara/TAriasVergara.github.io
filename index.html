About

I received a B.Sc. in Electronic Engineering from the University of Antioquia (UdeA, Colombia) in 2014, a Master of Science degree at the same institution in 2017, and a Ph.D. in Computer Science (summa cum laude) in 2022 through a bi-national joint program between UdeA and FAU. Since 2023 I am an associated researcher at the Pattern Recognition Lab (FAU, Erlangen), where I lead the Time Series Colloquium.

My early research focused on precision health multimodal applications, including detection and monitoring of Parkinson's disease with sensor and speech data, detection of cardiovascular diseases with ECG signals, and voice analysis with glottal waveforms extracted from video and electroglottography sensors. I have more than 100 journal and conference contributions resulting from my research experience, including a Marie Curie Early-Stage Researcher fellowship (2018-2021) for ML methods for pathological speech analysis, a postdoctoral position at University Hospital Erlangen (2021–2023) for AI methods to process high-speed videoendoscopy data of vocal folds, and, in 2024, I was a visiting scholar at the Gordon Center for Medical Imaging at Harvard Medical School where I investigated automatic methods to analyze vocal tract MRI sequences of tongue cancer patients.

My research has also extended to the analysis of animal speech; in particular, my team is developing machine learning (ML) and deep learning methods suitable for processing and analyzing wildlife data, automatically annotating vocalizations/acoustic events, and identifying individual animals to answer the following questions: Who is communicating? What information do animal vocalizations convey? How do their acoustic characteristics change across social contexts? and how can these signals be used to monitor animals over time?
